About us

It started with us.

Saffra was founded by a couple who found something rare with each other and wanted more couples to feel it.

This is the long version of why we are doing this. It takes about four minutes to read.

Chapter one

The feeling.

We will say the obvious part first. We are a couple, and we are close.

From early on, what we had was more than attraction. It was attention. We noticed each other. We made time when there was none. We learned that intimacy is mostly small things: a hand on a back in the kitchen, a look across a room, a real question at the end of a long day.

Being wanted by someone who truly sees you changes how you move through everything else. You are kinder at work. You are more patient with family. You are braver. We came to believe this feeling is one of the best things two people can give each other, and that it deserves to be looked after like anything else that matters.

Intimacy is mostly small things.

Chapter two

What we saw around us.

Then we looked around, and we kept hearing the same story from couples in good relationships. We are fine. We are just tired.

Work, children, phones, money, stress. Nobody had stopped loving anybody. They had stopped having room. Desire had not gone anywhere. It had been crowded out, a little more each month, until it felt like something that used to happen.

That stayed with us. What we had did not feel like luck. It felt like something we chose every day. We started asking what it would take to help other couples choose it too.

Chapter three

Why a gummy, of all things.

We are not doctors, and a gummy is not a relationship. We want to be clear about that before anything else.

But we do believe in rituals. A small thing you do together every day becomes a signal: this matters to us. So we went looking for something we could take ourselves, and we were disappointed by what we found. One kind of product promised a transformed night in thirty minutes. The other listed a dozen ingredients and would not say how much of any of them was inside.

We wanted the opposite. A focused list of ingredients. The amounts researchers have studied in people. A label that tells you everything. And a promise small enough to be true.

Nobody was making that, so we decided to.

Relationships

We are fine. We are just tired.

Ask a couple in a good relationship how things are and you will often hear the same answer. We are fine. We are just tired.

Nothing is broken. Nobody stopped loving anybody. What happened is quieter than that. Work got bigger, the house got louder, the phones came to bed, and the part of the day that used to belong to the two of you slowly filled with everything else.

Desire needs room

Desire rarely survives on leftovers. It tends to show up when there is a little energy, a little attention and a little time with nothing else in it. When all three run out, it does not die. It waits.

Four small things that make room

  1. Protect twenty minutes. Same time, most days. No screens, no logistics.
  2. Go to bed together. Even twice a week. Falling asleep at different times is one of the quietest ways couples drift.
  3. Touch without an agenda. A hand on a shoulder, a longer hug at the door. Small touch through the day makes closeness at night feel less like a leap.
  4. Say the tired part out loud. "I miss you and I am exhausted" is a much better sentence than silence.

None of this needs a product. If a daily gummy helps you remember to stop for a moment together, good. The stopping is what counts.

If the distance feels bigger than tiredness, or sex is painful or worrying, talk to a doctor or a couples therapist. That is what they are for.

Know your supplement

How to read a supplement label in 60 seconds

Most supplement labels are designed to be glanced at, not read. Here is how to read one properly in about a minute, whoever made it.

1. Look for the word "blend"

A "proprietary blend" lists ingredients but gives one total weight for all of them. You cannot tell if you are getting a useful amount of anything. If the label will not tell you the dose, assume it is small.

2. Check each amount against a study

An ingredient studied at 600 mg a day does very little at 50 mg. Search the ingredient name with the word "trial" and compare the numbers.

3. Look for the plant part and the extract

Good labels name the part of the plant, such as root, seed or stigma, and say what the extract is standardized to. Vague labels say "herb powder".

4. Ask who tested it

A brand testing its own product is not much of a test. Look for an independent lab and a report you can actually see for your batch.

5. Read the promise

Supplements can say they support normal body function. They cannot say they treat or cure anything. Words like "works in minutes" or "clinically proven" on a product that has never been in a trial are a warning sign.

6. Read the warnings

A careful brand tells you who should not take the product. No warnings at all usually means nobody thought about it.

Rituals

The ten-minute evening

Big gestures are lovely and rare. What changes a relationship is what happens on an ordinary Tuesday. This is a ten-minute routine you can start tonight.

Minute 1: phones away

Another room is best. Face down on the table does not count.

Minutes 2 to 4: the day, in three sentences each

One good thing, one hard thing, one thing you are looking forward to. No fixing, no advice. Just listen.

Minutes 5 to 7: one real question

Ask something you do not know the answer to. If you are stuck, our question deck has plenty.

Minutes 8 to 9: thank you

Name one specific thing the other person did today. "Thank you for handling the school run" lands better than "thanks for everything".

Minute 10: stay close

Sit near each other for one more minute without talking. It feels strange for about four days. Then it becomes the part you look forward to.

That is the whole ritual. Ten minutes, most nights, for a month. See what changes.

What it is

Saffron is the dried stigma of the crocus flower. Each flower grows just three of these red threads, and they are picked by hand. That is why saffron has been the world's most expensive spice for centuries.

Why people study it

Cooks have used saffron for color and flavor for a very long time. More recently, researchers have tested standardized saffron extracts in placebo-controlled trials, mostly looking at mood and sleep, and in smaller studies at desire and arousal.

Why the extract matters

Because saffron is so costly, it is one of the most commonly faked spices. A good extract names the plant part, is standardized to its active compounds, and comes with a lab report. We use 30 mg a day and print that number on the front of the jar.

What it will not do

Saffron is not a drug and it does not work in an evening. The trials measured daily use over weeks. You can see the studies, including the ones that found nothing, on our science page.

The product

What is in Saffra?

Seven ingredients in each formula. Both share saffron, fenugreek, vitamin B12 and vitamin D3. For Her adds L-theanine, vitamin B6 and folate. For Him adds zinc, selenium and vitamin E. Every amount is on the label.

Why two formulas?

The core is the same because the research on saffron and fenugreek covers women and men. The three extras are chosen for each.

Why gummies and not capsules?

Because a ritual you enjoy is one you keep. The trade-off is space, which is why we only include ingredients that fit at a useful amount.

Taking it

When should I take it?

One gummy in the morning and one in the evening, with or without food.

How long until I notice anything?

Give it four to eight weeks of daily use. That is what the studies measured. Some people notice nothing, which is why the first order has a 30-day money-back guarantee.

What if I miss a day?

Carry on the next day. Do not double up.

Safety

Who should not take it?

Anyone under 18, anyone pregnant, trying to conceive or nursing, and anyone with a peanut, chickpea or other legume allergy. If you take medication or have a medical condition, check with your doctor first.

Can I take it with my multivitamin?

Check the totals. Saffra already contains vitamins B12 and D3, plus B6 and folate in For Her, or zinc, selenium and vitamin E in For Him. Your pharmacist can tell you in a minute if the combined amounts are fine.

Is it a treatment for low libido or erectile problems?

No. Saffra is a dietary supplement. If something is worrying you, please see a doctor.

The two of you

My partner is not interested in trying this. What now?

Do not push. Try one of the free games or the ten-minute evening instead. The gummy is the least important part.

We have not been close in a long time. Where do we start?

Small. Go to bed at the same time twice this week, and ask one real question each night. If the distance feels heavy, a couples therapist is a good next step, not a last resort.
